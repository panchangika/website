Shanti Mantras (Peace Mantras)

From the Upanishads · Vedic prayers for peace

Read in: తెలుగు · தமிழ்

Four short peace prayers from the Upanishads, each ending with 'Om Shanti Shanti Shantih' — peace in body, in the world around us, and from forces beyond our control.

When to recite

At the start of study or any good work, before meals, at the end of prayers, and with children before they begin school work.

The stotram

Verse 1
oṃ saha nāvavatu | saha nau bhunaktu | saha vīryaṃ karavāvahai | tejasvi nāvadhītamastu mā vidviṣāvahai | oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||
Meaning: (Taittiriya Upanishad) May God protect us both, teacher and student. May he nourish us both. May we work together with energy. May our learning be bright and fruitful, and may we never dislike each other. Om, peace, peace, peace.
Verse 2
oṃ pūrṇamadaḥ pūrṇamidaṃ pūrṇātpūrṇamudacyate | pūrṇasya pūrṇamādāya pūrṇamevāvaśiṣyate || oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||
Meaning: (Isha Upanishad) That is complete, this is complete. From the complete comes the complete. Even when the complete is taken from the complete, the complete remains. Om, peace, peace, peace.
Verse 3
oṃ asato mā sadgamaya | tamaso mā jyotirgamaya | mṛtyormā amṛtaṃ gamaya | oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||
Meaning: (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad) Lead me from the unreal to the real. Lead me from darkness to light. Lead me from death to immortality. Om, peace, peace, peace.
Verse 4
oṃ sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ sarve santu nirāmayāḥ | sarve bhadrāṇi paśyantu mā kaścidduḥkhabhāgbhavet | oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ||
Meaning: (Traditional prayer) May everyone be happy. May everyone be free from illness. May everyone see only good things. May no one suffer. Om, peace, peace, peace.
