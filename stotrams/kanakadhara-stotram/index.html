Kanakadhara Stotram

Adi Shankaracharya · Hymn to Goddess Lakshmi

Read in: తెలుగు · தமிழ்

As a young boy seeking alms, Shankara met a very poor woman who gave him her only food — a single dried amla fruit. Moved by her kindness, he sang this hymn to Lakshmi, and the story says a 'stream of gold' (kanaka-dhara) of golden amlas rained on her house.

When to recite

Fridays, Akshaya Tritiya, Varalakshmi Vratam, Deepavali and every evening at lamp-lighting time.

Meaning in simple words

Shankara describes Lakshmi's glance falling on Vishnu like a bee on a dark tamala tree, and prays that the same loving glance, full of compassion, fall on him and bring prosperity. He compares her to lightning shining on the dark cloud of Vishnu's chest, to the moon's sister born from the ocean, and to the lotus-dweller who gives joy to Vishnu. He asks her glance to rain like a monsoon cloud on the dry field of a poor devotee and wash away the heat of poverty. Later verses bow to her as Saraswati, Gauri and the power of creation, preservation and dissolution, and as the giver of wealth, children and fame. The closing verses say that those who recite this hymn daily become rich in virtue and blessed by Lakshmi.

Verse 2
mugdhā muhurvidadhatī vadane murāreḥ prematrapāpraṇihitāni gatāgatāni | mālā dṛśormadhukarīva mahotpale yā sā me śriyaṃ diśatu sāgarasambhavāyāḥ || 2 ||
Verse 3

Verse 4

Verse 5

Verse 6

Verse 7
prāptaṃ padaṃ prathamataḥ khalu yatprabhāvā- nmāṅgalyabhāji madhumāthini manmathena | mayyāpatettadiha mantharamīkṣaṇārdhaṃ mandālasaṃ ca makarālayakanyakāyāḥ || 7 ||
Verse 8
dadyāddayānupavano draviṇāmbudhārā- masminnakiñcanavihaṅgaśiśau viṣaṇṇe | duṣkarmagharmamapanīya cirāya dūraṃ nārāyaṇapraṇayinīnayanāmbuvāhaḥ || 8 ||
Verse 9

Verse 10

Verse 12

Verse 16

Verse 17
