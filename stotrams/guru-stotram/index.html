Traditional (from the Guru Gita tradition) · Prayer to the Guru

Well-loved verses that bow to the Guru as Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva — the one who opens our eyes with the light of knowledge.

When to recite

Thursdays, Guru Purnima, before lessons with a teacher, and at the start of any class or recital.

The stotram

Verse 1
gururbrahmā gururviṣṇuḥ gururdevo maheśvaraḥ | guruḥ sākṣāt paraṃ brahma tasmai śrīgurave namaḥ || 1 ||
Meaning: The Guru is Brahma, the Guru is Vishnu, the Guru is the god Maheshvara. The Guru is truly the Supreme Brahman itself. Salutations to that revered Guru.
Verse 2
akhaṇḍamaṇḍalākāraṃ vyāptaṃ yena carācaram | tatpadaṃ darśitaṃ yena tasmai śrīgurave namaḥ || 2 ||
Meaning: Salutations to the Guru who showed me that state — the undivided whole that fills everything, moving and unmoving.
Verse 3
ajñānatimirāndhasya jñānāñjanaśalākayā | cakṣurunmīlitaṃ yena tasmai śrīgurave namaḥ || 3 ||
Meaning: I was blind in the darkness of ignorance; salutations to the Guru who opened my eyes with the healing stick of knowledge.
Verse 4
dhyānamūlaṃ gurormūrtiḥ pūjāmūlaṃ guroḥ padam | mantramūlaṃ gurorvākyaṃ mokṣamūlaṃ guroḥ kṛpā || 4 ||
Meaning: The Guru's form is the root of meditation; the Guru's feet are the root of worship; the Guru's word is the root of mantra; the Guru's grace is the root of liberation.
