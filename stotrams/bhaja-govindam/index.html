Bhaja Govindam

Adi Shankaracharya (Moha Mudgaram) · Verses on what truly matters in life

Read in: తెలుగు · தமிழ்

The story goes that in Kashi, Shankara saw an old scholar memorising grammar rules, and told him: 'Worship Govinda, you fool — grammar will not save you when death comes.' The verses are a loving wake-up call about wealth, youth, family and time.

When to recite

Ekadashi, Saturdays, Vaikuntha Ekadashi, and any quiet evening of reflection.

Meaning in simple words

Worship Govinda, for rules of grammar will not save you at the time of death. Give up the thirst for wealth; be content with what comes from honest work. Do not be proud of wealth, friends or youth — time takes them all away in a moment. Life is as unsteady as a water drop on a lotus leaf. Your family loves you while you earn; when the body grows old, no one asks after you. Day and night, morning and evening, winter and spring come and go; time plays and life wears away, yet the wind of desire never leaves. Who is your wife, who is your son? This world is very strange. Keep the company of good people — from good company comes detachment, from detachment freedom from delusion, and from that, liberation. Read the Gita, sing Vishnu's names, meditate on Lakshmi's Lord, serve the poor. Hold on to the feet of the Guru, and you will see God dwelling in your own heart.
